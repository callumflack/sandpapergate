## Transcript

<p id="p-001" class="source-passage">SARAH FERGUSON, PRESENTER: Usman Khawaja, welcome to 7.30.</p>

<p id="p-002" class="source-passage">USMAN KHAWAJA, FORMER TEST CRICKETER: Thanks for having me.</p>

<p id="p-003" class="source-passage">SARAH FERGUSON: Dave Warner has come out and backed your account of the ball tampering scandal in South Africa. In doing so, he said, "Lies will eventually get out." What lies is he talking about?</p>

<p id="p-004" class="source-passage">USMAN KHAWAJA: I mean, I'm not going speak on behalf of Davy. I've tried my best to tell my story in an open and safe environment. Twenty-two pages is, is what sandpaper is and I've tried my best out of, in that to explain my version of events.</p>

<p id="p-005" class="source-passage">There's no easy way to do it, but I mean, I'm not here to inflame the situation anymore. I was here to tell my truth, what I saw, what I felt, and to stick with those three guys that were made out to be the only people who have taken it, and I wanted that to be shown.</p>

<p id="p-006" class="source-passage">SARAH FERGUSON: Now, the bowling group of Pat Cummins, Hazlewood, Starc, and Lyon denied that they knew in advance about the use of sandpaper in that Cape Town match. To your knowledge, is that untrue that they did not know?</p>

<p id="p-007" class="source-passage">USMAN KHAWAJA: I'll say again, I've given my statement about what happened the game before and what was called back then Port Elizabeth and where we played there and what happened. And I was honest to what I saw and what conversations I had.</p>

<p id="p-008" class="source-passage">You know, it's never been for me about pointing fingers and saying, "He did this, he said that." And that's why even when I first wrote my edit, there was names, there was lots of things, I pulled it back.</p>

<p id="p-009" class="source-passage">Sometimes I'm very specific on things and sometimes they're a bit vague on things and I do it on purpose because I'm trying to tell a story.</p>

<p id="p-010" class="source-passage">But my main objective here is always to try to take accountability as a team. To say, yes, this was happening. And even though that one person did it or the captain knew about it and David Warner always took the heat too for it, that it wasn't just them, that we were all involved in it as a team, as a squad that was there in South Africa.</p>

<p id="p-011" class="source-passage">And that's what I'm trying to project because it's never black and white and what I've written there shows what I saw and I'm happy to stand by it.</p>

<p id="p-012" class="source-passage">SARAH FERGUSON: Well, let's get a sense then of what you actually saw. Were those players that I mentioned involved in conversations about ball tampering?</p>

<p id="p-013" class="source-passage">USMAN KHAWAJA: I'm just not going, I'm not going to entertain that. And I say this for one reason. I wrote it in my book because it was a space, safe space for me to write.</p>

<p id="p-014" class="source-passage">I took a long time. It took me a long time to first write the chapter, then go over it and try to work out a way that it was as balanced as possible. I did it the best way I could.</p>

<p id="p-015" class="source-passage">At the same time, trying to be open and honest about what I saw and how things were. I implore people. It's hard for me to talk about this with you to get the right context. There's so much happening at that time. And I felt like I found a nice balance with it at the end of it.</p>

<p id="p-016" class="source-passage">And I learned from this week when the media article came out that whenever things come out in the media, they get sensationalised all over the, they're all everywhere and the clickbait, it just runs rapid.</p>

<p id="p-017" class="source-passage">So if I say to anyone, and I just keep going, “I've talked on social media about this and I've written in my book. That's my statement. That's what it is. And I'm not going to really speak on any more of it at this moment in time."</p>

<p id="p-018" class="source-passage">SARAH FERGUSON: You say you want to take the responsibility away from being landed on just those three people. That means that other people must take responsibility as well, which takes me back to that question. I want to put it very clearly to you so there can be no doubt about what it is you are saying. Did Australian captain Pat Cummins have prior knowledge that sandpaper was being used to change the surface of the ball?</p>

<p id="p-019" class="source-passage">USMAN KHAWAJA: I'm not going to answer that. I've written what I've written in my book. I've come out and stood by those three guys and told my account of what I saw. I think Chadd Sayers came out the other day and I'm not going to speculate on behalf of anyone else and I've been very clear of that even when I written the book.</p>

<p id="p-020" class="source-passage">There are instances where I talk about us and them, but I've been very clear not naming names and staying out of it. So I'm not going to start now.</p>

<p id="p-021" class="source-passage">SARAH FERGUSON: And yet by writing this account, Usman Khawaja, you are naming names because you are saying that it's not just those three players who were responsible. So you are saying the rest of the playing group and the coaches had prior knowledge of ball tampering in that series, both in the test where they were caught and in the prior test.</p>

<p id="p-022" class="source-passage">USMAN KHAWAJA: Are you talking about ball tampering or sandpaper?</p>

<p id="p-023" class="source-passage">SARAH FERGUSON: Sandpaper.</p>

<p id="p-024" class="source-passage">USMAN KHAWAJA: I mean, I've written in my account from what I've seen. I've told you again, time and time again, like the easiest way for me to do it and people want to know the answers and lots of different answers.</p>

<p id="p-025" class="source-passage">And I've left it open-ended to some respects because I've told my story and I've told my truth and I'll speak for myself and there's other times where I'm talking about what's happened in that situation, but this has never been a witch hunt for me.</p>

<p id="p-026" class="source-passage">It's only been those three guys who've had to cop this flack for a long time. I'm hoping other people will come up and talk about it honestly. That's what I'm hoping but I'm not forcing anyone to do anything.</p>

<p id="p-027" class="source-passage">And you got to remember, Pat Cummins, Pat Cummins is a good friend of mine, Mitchell Starc, a lot of these guys, like the guys on the team, other players in the team, Shaun Marsh, Mitch Marsh, they're all very good friends of mine.</p>

<p id="p-028" class="source-passage">I'm not here to go on a witch hunt. All I've ever wanted is for us to take accountability as a team and I don't think that's a lot to say. I'm not asking a lot from people and I say, "We were a part of it, that was us. Let's just take accountability and tell your story. Don't have to tell your story." But I felt for those three guys that kept having to share the load for the whole time.</p>

<p id="p-029" class="source-passage">SARAH FERGUSON: Is there a venue, Usman Kawaja, where you would answer these questions like a much more fulsome investigation by Cricket Australia? Is that what you are calling for?</p>

<p id="p-030" class="source-passage">USMAN KHAWAJA: I'm not calling for anything. I'm literally sharing my version of the truth. I mean, this is a big part of my life too, because a lot of people that, you know, that have been affected by this, I understand that, but there's a lot of people that, you know, whether the bowlers or the other batsmen or the 12th men, everyone says how hard this was on their families.</p>

<p id="p-031" class="source-passage">It was hard on my family too. It was hard on me too. This is not an easy conversation to have. I mean, I'm human too. I had three or four months where I really struggled with it, a whole year after. I mean, I struggled with it even till now, I struggled with it.</p>

<p id="p-032" class="source-passage">And even till this day, I'm still struggling with it, but I don't believe I'm the actual victim. I believe those three guys, as I said, in my social media, and I'll stand by them.</p>

<p id="p-033" class="source-passage">SARAH FERGUSON: You say you wanted the team at the time to take collective responsibility. Now that could have resulted, obviously, in the whole team being banned from playing test cricket for a while. We saw what happened to Steve Smith. He got a very long ban. Would a ban for the whole team have actually been appropriate at that time?</p>

<p id="p-034" class="source-passage">USMAN KHAWAJA: I don't think the ban for the three, I don't think the ban for the three players was appropriate.</p>

<p id="p-035" class="source-passage">SARAH FERGUSON: And what about for the whole team? You say you wanted the whole team to take collective responsibility. Would a ban for the whole team have been the appropriate thing to happen at that time?</p>

<p id="p-036" class="source-passage">USMAN KHAWAJA: I don't believe the whole team would have got a ban if we went in together. I don't think we would have. That's why I wanted collective.</p>

<p id="p-037" class="source-passage">I think it would have been shared. It's very hard to ban the whole team.</p>

<p id="p-038" class="source-passage">SARAH FERGUSON: Do you believe that Cricket Australia perpetrated a coverup to prevent there being a wider ban across the team?</p>

<p id="p-039" class="source-passage">USMAN KHAWAJA: I don't feel like it was a thorough investigation because I never got investigated and I was sitting next to Cameron Bancroft the whole time. So it's hard for me to say coverup, but I will say it felt rushed.</p>

<p id="p-040" class="source-passage">SARAH FERGUSON: A question on why now, have you timed these revelations to promote sales of your book?</p>

<p id="p-041" class="source-passage">USMAN KHAWAJA: I mean, a lot of people have put up a narrative that I'm somehow gaining from this, but at no stage was sandpaper ever discussed in 2023 when I signed my book deal.</p>

<p id="p-042" class="source-passage">My manager didn't even know about it until six months ago. Neither did anyone really at Penguin. All they said to me was write your story. Whether I wrote about sandpaper or not would not have made one bit of difference. I could have written anything I want.</p>

<p id="p-043" class="source-passage">Even when I gave it to them six months ago, they were shocked, because I don't really talk about this to anyone. I do not talk about this. And when I wrote it, they were shocked. So anyone who says that I've used this to gain from my book, not at all.</p>

<p id="p-044" class="source-passage">I could have written anything I wanted in my book. I had even right to the very end till the deadline came. I could have taken it out and they couldn't say a word, but I chose to put it in there.</p>

<p id="p-045" class="source-passage">SARAH FERGUSON: Are you concerned about the impact of these revelations just ahead of the first test of the summer?</p>

<p id="p-046" class="source-passage">USMAN KHAWAJA: No, and I'm going to be really honest. The reason I felt more comfortable saying it now is because I don't speak for the team anymore.</p>

<p id="p-047" class="source-passage">I mean, if I try to come out and do it while I'm still on the team, they have to look at me every day. We have to go operate as a team. I don't think it's ever possible to do that.</p>

<p id="p-048" class="source-passage">I don't speak for the team anymore. They don't have to see my face. They don't have to see me around. They can move on. I'm away. They're not around me anymore. So I think it's a lot easier for them to actually go out and play cricket when I'm not on the team, because I don't speak for them. I don't influence them anymore.</p>

<p id="p-049" class="source-passage">SARAH FERGUSON: I want to just move on to a different subject. You say you were not made captain after Steve Smith was stood down because of your colour. What made you reach that conclusion?</p>

<p id="p-050" class="source-passage">USMAN KHAWAJA: No, I mean, these are, this, again, there's no concrete evidence of that in terms of what I heard and as I wrote in my book.</p>

<p id="p-051" class="source-passage">As I explained with anything in life, whenever I talk about racism or anything along those lines, it's how I feel at the time. It's what things, and then everyone's like, oh, they have different perspectives of it, but, yes, but that's how I felt.</p>

<p id="p-052" class="source-passage">And I caveat that at the same time with a year later when Tim Paine was captain, I look back on it and I think, actually, you know what? Tim Paine was a great captain. And so, I'm like, I'm not really sure if they made a poor decision there.</p>

<p id="p-053" class="source-passage">At the time, I felt a bit aggrieved, and in my book, I'm trying to explain to the reader. That's how I felt at the time. But when I look back on it, I actually don't think it was the wrong decision. I just, that's how I felt.</p>

<p id="p-054" class="source-passage">SARAH FERGUSON: But did you think there was an element of race in the decision?</p>

<p id="p-055" class="source-passage">USMAN KHAWAJA: I can never say for certain, but I mean, it felt like, as I've said, explained in my book, the stereotypes that were used against me throughout my career, about not training hard enough, not fitting in, not working hard, being lazy.</p>

<p id="p-056" class="source-passage">I think these dogged me throughout my whole career, so not necessarily for that decision, but I think it was something that I explained my book quite openly of how I felt.</p>

<p id="p-057" class="source-passage">SARAH FERGUSON: And did you ever stop feeling like an outsider in Australian cricket?</p>

<p id="p-058" class="source-passage">USMAN KHAWAJA: And I very much, yeah. I think this, the moment that in 2022 at the end of the series when we won the Ashes and I ran, I always used to go off the field when they used to spray alcohol and then Pat Cummins called me back, was the first time in my life, I felt really a part of the Australian cricket team.</p>

<p id="p-059" class="source-passage">That's why I've always had so much love for Patty for that reason. I told him that after the game and it was something, I'm not sure he realised at the time what it meant to me, but even when I talk about it now, it's a very emotional time, a very emotional feeling for me because I still remember, it was like, wow, like, it felt so good. So I've always had a special place with that in my heart.</p>

<p id="p-060" class="source-passage">SARAH FERGUSON: It's a tricky thing you say with Pat Cummins because reading the book, there is only one conclusion that a reader can draw, and that is that Pat Cummins knew about the use of sandpaper. Is this going to damage your friendship with Pat Cummins?</p>

<p id="p-061" class="source-passage">USMAN KHAWAJA: I'm not sure. I'm not sure. But I've been friends with Pat a long time. I've told my truth and I've given my reasons at the end of the day. I think some things I've always stood for, my values and what I think is right, and yeah, there's no ideal way of doing this, but I can't speak for Pat.</p>

<p id="p-062" class="source-passage">SARAH FERGUSON: Usman Khawaja, thank you very much indeed for joining us and congratulations on the publication of the book. Thank you.</p>

<p id="p-063" class="source-passage">USMAN KHAWAJA: Thank you.</p>
