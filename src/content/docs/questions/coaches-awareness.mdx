## The claim and its scope

Presence at a discussion, knowing of the plan, encouraging it and learning of it after the television exposure are different propositions. A claim about one coach does not establish the knowledge of all staff.

This page presents archive analysis based on the cited sources. It distinguishes what a witness says, what an institution found and what can be inferred from either.

## Arguments for the claim

### Bancroft identifies a coach and a specific instruction

He identifies Saker as the coach who told him to do more during the Cape Town innings and recalls playing and coaching staff acknowledging his work at Port Elizabeth. These are allegations of prior encouragement, not merely awareness afterwards.

**Limit:** These are later allegations. Saker’s May2021 broad-blame comments and June2021 explicit personal denial are both now collected; neither is a response to Bancroft’s specific2026 instruction allegation. His complete current response and direct corroboration are still needed.

### The alleged discussion was wider than two players

Bancroft says Saker and Lehmann were present as the sandpaper proposal developed and recalls people agreeing. He also describes Hutchison objecting to what was happening at a later team discussion.

**Limit:** He expressly cannot speak for every word shared, says people moved in and out, and says Hutchison deserves her own account. Physical presence does not by itself establish knowledge or endorsement.

## Arguments against or limiting the claim

### Smith’s original account excluded the coaches

At the initial press conference Smith said the coaches were not involved and attributed the decision to the leadership group. This is a direct competing account of who participated.

**Limit:** CA later charged him with misleading public comments about the nature and participants of the plan; the initial exclusion cannot be treated as independently verified.

### Post-exposure instructions cannot alone prove earlier knowledge

Bancroft recalls Handscomb relaying Lehmann’s instruction to remove the sandpaper after he had been caught and infers Lehmann must already have known. The timing also permits knowledge acquired from the newly exposed footage.

**Limit:** This timing objection narrows the inference from that incident; it does not answer Bancroft’s separate allegations about earlier changing-room discussions.

### Lehmann’s contemporary denial and later recollection both remain on record

The newly collected official 2018 report reproduces Lehmann’s explicit denial of prior knowledge, omitted from the other published transcript in this archive. In ABC’s 2026 coverage he disputes universal knowledge; on the collective-responsibility meeting he says he does not remember and cannot confirm or deny it.

General knowledge that ball tampering exists and acceptance of cultural responsibility are not admissions of knowing this sandpaper plan. ### Hick expressly denies knowing of discussions at either Test

In his statement to the original newspaper, Hick says he did not hear or know of discussions about tampering at Port Elizabeth or Cape Town and first learned of it from the big-screen footage. This directly contests a literal claim that every staff member knew beforehand.

**Limit:** The capture is the complete published report with his quoted statement, not a raw interview recording. His denial is a participant’s competing account; it does not independently settle other staff members’ knowledge.

### Saker’s later 2021 response explicitly denies sandpaper knowledge

The June3 report quotes Saker saying he had no idea sandpaper was involved and understood the team to be using normal reverse-swing tactics. That is an express personal denial, more specific than his May remarks about a monumental mistake and many people to blame. Both belong in the record.

**Limit:** This is a contemporary publisher report with selected direct quotations, not the full underlying conference or verified audio. It predates the specific2026 allegations and does not establish another coach’s knowledge.

### Saker’s 2019 public denial concerns condoning cheating

In the original May 2019 interview report, Saker says the team had never condoned cheating. This is an earlier public denial relevant to the encouragement part of the claim, alongside his separate June 2021 denial of sandpaper knowledge.

**Limit:** Not condoning, not knowing and preventing an incident are different propositions. The report contains selected interview quotations and predates the specific 2026 allegations; it does not establish what Saker privately knew or answer the later alleged instruction directly.

## What would help resolve it

- Obtain the complete underlying Saker conference and full original Hick response, plus Hutchison and Handscomb accounts where available. Hick’s published paragraphs do not establish whether he responded in writing or orally. Lehmann’s full ABC episode and official2018 materials are collected; exact speech remains unverified.
- What did each person hear before the cameras exposed the incident?
- Do contemporary staff communications, audio or investigation records corroborate the alleged instructions?
