## What Khawaja alleges

Khawaja says the three bans were inappropriate and he does not believe the whole team would have been banned had they gone in together, reasoning that banning an entire team would be difficult.

**Source boundary:** This is his predicted alternative, not a historical decision. It is not a demand for identical punishment for everyone.

## Strongest case for the claim

### Other participants question the severity and value of individualising the response

Paine thinks collective ownership might have worked out better for the three. Cummins regards their punishment as excessive. The ICC sanctions were substantially lower than CA’s, providing a concrete proportionality comparison.

**Limit:** The ICC and CA applied different codes and remits. Agreement that sanctions were excessive does not establish what CA would have done after a different admission.

## Strongest challenge or limit

### No alternative decision or controlling reason establishes the predicted outcome

CA justified sanctions by integrity and reputational harm. A shared statement could have widened exposure, changed the evidence or left the individual charges intact. The archive contains no alternative disciplinary ruling showing that team ownership would prevent or reduce bans.

**Limit:** That makes the prediction untested, not impossible. The specific admissions, applicable powers and complete reasons would be needed to assess it properly.

## Where the record stands

A plausible but untested counterfactual. The proportionality dispute has concrete comparative material; the prediction about a whole-team response is materially less established.

This is an assessment of the captured record, not an adjudicated finding.
