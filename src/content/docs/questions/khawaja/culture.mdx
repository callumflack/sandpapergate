## What Khawaja alleges

Khawaja describes earlier ball-working methods and says sandpaper was new on that South African tour. Brettig summarises the book’s account of competitive pressure. Khawaja also reports what he heard about South Africa from another player.

**Source boundary:** The other-team claim is hearsay. His experience of Australian teams is not knowledge of every team everywhere. His characterisation of methods as prohibited is not an independent historical rules ruling.

## Strongest case for the claim

### Several accounts and the cultural review support a broader problem

Paine’s 2022 interview describes widespread tampering and excessive competitive culture. Lehmann discusses broad ball-tampering practices while distinguishing individual knowledge. The Ethics Centre identifies institutional cultural failures.

**Limit:** Convergence about culture does not prove the same incident, method, legality or individual awareness. It can explain conditions without excusing deliberate acts.

## Strongest challenge or limit

### Permitted management and prohibited alteration must not be collapsed

Cummins describes the team’s intended methods as legal; Saker denies condoning cheating. Those positions require examining the actual method and governing rules rather than assuming all work on a ball is equivalent.

**Limit:** They do not establish that every method was lawful or disprove a specific illegal act. The disputed automatic legal/illegal wording remains open; this analysis uses the published report and makes no new ruling under 2018 law.

## Where the record stands

There is substantial convergence on a broader cultural problem. The legality and causal role of each practice still require event-specific evidence and the applicable historical rule. Culture alone cannot decide who knew of sandpaper.

This is an assessment of the captured record, not an adjudicated finding.

## Open questions

- Which practices occurred, when, and under which exact rule?
- How did competitive pressure affect specific decisions rather than merely accompany them?
- What distinguishes tolerated methods, concealed violations and the sandpaper escalation?
