## What Khawaja alleges

Khawaja frames his intervention as justice for the sanctioned three and says the team should have owned what happened together.

**Source boundary:** This is a moral claim with factual premises. Wider cultural responsibility, knowing participation, equal blame and equal sanctions are different propositions.

## Strongest case for the claim

### Wider participation and institutional culture support a wider response

If more people knowingly participated, a three-person factual account is incomplete. Separately, the Ethics Centre assigns responsibility for cultural failure to CA; Lehmann accepts responsibility for team culture while denying prior knowledge. Paine later regrets the team’s limited support and wonders whether joint ownership would have helped.

**Limit:** The cultural review is not a reinvestigation proving every person knew about sandpaper. Shared moral regret does not prove a specific meeting or instruction occurred.

## Strongest challenge or limit

### Specific individual acts still warrant differentiated responsibility

CA differentiates Smith’s knowledge and failure to prevent, Warner’s development and instructions, and Bancroft’s implementation and concealment. A common benefit or failed culture does not erase those acts. Nor were the three the only people to face any consequence: Lehmann resigned and CA underwent a cultural review.

**Limit:** Those wider consequences do not settle their adequacy or whether the three bore a disproportionate burden. Khawaja does not demand identical bans; the moral remedy needs to be specified.

## Where the record stands

Broader cultural responsibility has substantial institutional and participant support. What justice required beyond it remains a reasoned moral judgment: acknowledgement, differentiated sanctions, institutional change and personal support need separate consideration.

This is an assessment of the captured record, not an adjudicated finding.

## Open questions

- What should a shared account have admitted about each person’s conduct?
- What form of responsibility is sought: public acknowledgement, sanction, institutional reform or support?
- How should responsibility reflect knowledge, power, action and opportunity to intervene?
