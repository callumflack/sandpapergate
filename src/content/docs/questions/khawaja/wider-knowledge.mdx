## What Khawaja alleges

Khawaja’s original interview says the team knew and should respond together. This narrower proposition follows from his broader allegation; it is not a substitute for assessing his claim that everyone knew.

**Source boundary:** Define the match and the knowledge claimed: awareness of actual use is different from knowledge of a particular future implementation plan.

## Strongest case for the claim

### Sayers admits his own knowledge and handling

If Sayers’s account of knowing about sandpaper and helping remove it is true, at least one additional squad member knew. That establishes a narrower proposition without first proving what each frontline bowler knew. Khawaja also places himself beside Bancroft and asserts awareness of relevant events.

**Limit:** Sayers’s Port Elizabeth involvement does not by itself establish knowledge of a particular Cape Town plan. His further inference about everyone is a separate claim.

## Strongest challenge or limit

### The official finding was explicitly confined to three

CA’s public finding said prior knowledge of the Cape Town incident was confined to Smith, Warner and Bancroft. That is a materially conflicting institutional account.

**Limit:** The underlying report and witness evidence are unavailable here. Its incident-specific scope cannot settle every act on the tour, and later first-person admissions must be assessed rather than excluded merely because they conflict with it.

## Where the record stands

Wider-than-three knowledge has a stronger affirmative case than universal knowledge, especially through Sayers’s self-admission. A failure to prove that everyone knew would not establish that only three knew.

This is an assessment of the captured record, not an adjudicated finding.

## Open questions

- Who knew of sandpaper specifically, at which match and before which event?
- How does the official finding’s precise remit relate to the later Port Elizabeth admissions?
- What did Khawaja personally hear, see or say before the exposure?
