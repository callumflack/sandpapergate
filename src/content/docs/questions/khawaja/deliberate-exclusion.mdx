## What Khawaja alleges

In the original November 7 interview Khawaja explicitly says he feels this was why he was not interviewed. The later ABC answer stops short of calling the process a cover-up and describes it as rushed.

**Source boundary:** Both statements belong in the record. The later qualification must not erase the stronger original motive allegation; the original belief must not become an established finding.

## Strongest case for the claim

### His claimed proximity, stance and omission make the suspicion concrete

Khawaja says he sat beside Bancroft, advocated a shared response and waited for an interview that never came. Sayers alleges another potentially relevant non-interview. Those facts, if accepted, are compatible with selective avoidance.

**Limit:** Compatibility is not proof of motive. No cited witness supplies investigator communications explaining a decision to avoid inconvenient testimony.

## Strongest challenge or limit

### The record does not discriminate between deliberate avoidance and other explanations

Haste, a narrow remit, reliance on incomplete answers or poor witness selection could also produce omissions. Bancroft admits withholding facts. Khawaja’s later cover-up qualification itself limits how firmly he puts the intentional-concealment case.

**Limit:** These alternatives are possible explanations, not established excuses. CA’s general assurances do not independently resolve intent either. Sayers’s speculation about reasons cannot supply the missing investigator evidence.

## Where the record stands

This serious intentional-act allegation has much thinner support than the non-interview claim. The archive has circumstantial suspicion, not evidence establishing why investigators selected or omitted witnesses.

This is an assessment of the captured record, not an adjudicated finding.

## Open questions

- Who selected witnesses, and what did they know of Khawaja’s stance?
- Is there a contemporary record explaining why he was not interviewed?
- What evidence would distinguish a flawed or hurried inquiry from deliberate avoidance?
