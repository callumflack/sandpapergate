## What Khawaja alleges

In the original interview Khawaja recounts asking the team to take responsibility together after the game. His ABC answers reiterate that stance.

**Source boundary:** The existence and content of a proposal must be separated from whether it was right and whether it would have changed sanctions.

## Strongest case for the claim

### Other accounts recall discussions about the response

Sayers recalls a lengthy discussion after that day’s play and, when prompted, vaguely remembers resistance by two players. Bancroft describes a lengthy players-and-staff meeting after the match about the public response.

**Limit:** The after-day and after-match accounts may describe different meetings. Sayers does not name the objectors. Bancroft’s description does not independently establish every word of Khawaja’s proposal.

## Strongest challenge or limit

### Cummins cannot recall the proposed collective response

Cummins says he does not remember a conversation about collective responsibility or Khawaja standing up to advocate it. Lehmann neither confirms nor denies the meeting, referring to multiple rooms and meetings and his contact with CA.

**Limit:** Failure to remember is weaker than a categorical denial. Paine’s 2022 denial of a prospective planning meeting concerns what the team would do to the ball, not necessarily a later meeting about responsibility.

## Where the record stands

There is affirmative testimony for a proposal and partial support for response meetings, but the chronology, attendance and exact proposal remain unresolved. Neither general regret nor a denial about a different meeting settles this event.

This is an assessment of the captured record, not an adjudicated finding.

## Open questions

- Which meeting, on which day, is each witness recalling?
- Was the proposal a common factual confession, a shared public statement, equal penalties or support for the three?
- Who heard it, how did they respond, and is any contemporaneous record available?
