## What Khawaja alleges

The November 7 report says an exchange with an unnamed coach helped set events off. ABC later reports the book’s words about doing more. This establishes the initiating Khawaja allegation without silently naming the coach for him.

**Source boundary:** The complete chapter is absent. These are attributed book summaries or selected quotations, rather than a captured full first-person narration.

## Strongest case for the claim

### Bancroft later identifies Saker and the instruction

Bancroft expressly names Saker as the coach who told him to do more at Cape Town. He separately alleges praise from Saker at Port Elizabeth. His specific account supports the claimed exchange more directly than generic talk of a permissive culture.

**Limit:** The Port Elizabeth praise is a different event. The later testimony is retrospective and does not independently authenticate every word of Khawaja’s earlier account.

## Strongest challenge or limit

### The words require a link to sandpaper knowledge

An instruction to do more of whatever is making the ball swing could encourage activity without showing knowledge of the material. Saker’s explicit 2021 sandpaper-knowledge denial conflicts with informed encouragement if the instruction involved known sandpaper. His broad-blame comments do not concede that link.

**Limit:** The older denial predates the specific allegation. Cummins’s lack of recollection and Lehmann’s unawareness are not eyewitness refutations. Hick’s personal denial does not establish what another coach said.
