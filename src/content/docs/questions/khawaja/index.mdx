Khawaja’s November 7 account reopened the dispute about sandpaper in South Africa in 2018. Its factual allegations, plea for shared accountability and predictions about different sanctions carry different burdens. This dossier assesses the captured record, without treating two opposing columns as equally strong evidence.

## Where to begin

The participant testimony for **earlier use** and **knowledge beyond the sanctioned three** is stronger than the evidence that **every player and staff member knew**. An incomplete inquiry and deliberate avoidance of a witness are also different claims: Khawaja alleges the latter in his original interview, while his later ABC answer is more qualified about a cover-up. The pages below preserve both.

## Read the allegation before the argument

The [original November 7 interview](/documents/sp055-usman-khawaja-s-original-november-2026-newspaper-interview/) mixes direct Khawaja quotations with Brettig’s summaries of the book. The [full ABC publisher transcript](/documents/sp024-khawaja-abc-7-30-interview/) distinguishes Ferguson’s questions from Khawaja’s answers. Neither substitutes for the complete Sabr chapter, which remains missing. The 2018 notes are reported to exist; their contents and provenance have not been examined here.

Bancroft and Sayers provide later participant accounts. They do not become Khawaja’s words, and repeated coverage of one account is not an additional witness. Published transcript passages are used here; [direct listening checks remain open](/about/recording-checks/).

## Use and knowledge

| Specific claim | Where the captured record stands |
| --- | --- |
| [Sandpaper was used at Port Elizabeth](/questions/khawaja/earlier-use/) | Earlier onset has comparatively substantial participant support in this record. It remains a retrospective account without captured contemporary physical or broadcast confirmation. Duration is more disputed than onset. |
| [Knowledge extended beyond the three sanctioned players](/questions/khawaja/wider-knowledge/) | Wider-than-three knowledge has a stronger affirmative case than universal knowledge, especially through Sayers’s self-admission. A failure to prove that everyone knew would not establish that only three knew. |
| [“All of us”: every player and staff member knew](/questions/khawaja/everyone-knew/) | The captured record contains direct self-implication by Sayers, Khawaja’s broad assertion and reported observations, and Bancroft’s much more specific allegations against named people. That makes wider-than-three knowledge a materially stronger case than universal knowledge. The strongest test of broad disclosure is Bancroft’s alleged Port Elizabeth day-three discussion explicitly about sandpaper, not the assumption that bowlers must know. The sharpest named coaching conflict is Saker; the bowlers’ swing feedback still admits a competing interpretation. Hick is included by the universal allegation without a named contrary episode in these passages. The denials remain live, and the captured record does not establish every individual’s pre-exposure knowledge. None of this turns shared knowledge into identical participation or blame. |
| [A coach encouraged Bancroft to do more](/questions/khawaja/coach-encouragement/) | There is specific additional participant support for the exchange; the speaker’s knowledge and exact context remain decisive. General cultural responsibility, encouragement and knowing sandpaper instruction carry different burdens. |
| [Smith heard the earlier discussion and distanced himself](/questions/khawaja/smith-response/) | Cape Town knowledge and failure to prevent are specified in CA’s public charges. The earlier exchange has later participant support but remains unverified against a contemporary record. Neither operational authorship nor effective intervention follows from distancing alone. |

## Response and accountability

| Specific claim | Where the captured record stands |
| --- | --- |
| [Khawaja tried to delay the immediate press conference](/questions/khawaja/press-conference/) | Partly consistent later testimony supports a timing disagreement. Khawaja’s specific role has limited corroboration; any claim that waiting would have improved the outcome is counterfactual. |
| [Khawaja urged a shared response after exposure](/questions/khawaja/collective-meeting/) | There is affirmative testimony for a proposal and partial support for response meetings, but the chronology, attendance and exact proposal remain unresolved. Neither general regret nor a denial about a different meeting settles this event. |
| [The investigation missed relevant witnesses](/questions/khawaja/incomplete-inquiry/) | The claimed non-interviews raise a substantial procedural question. The completeness judgment cannot be settled from public assurances or later recollections alone; remit, witness selection and treatment of conflicting evidence matter. |
| [Khawaja alleges deliberate exclusion from the inquiry](/questions/khawaja/deliberate-exclusion/) | This serious intentional-act allegation has much thinner support than the non-interview claim. The archive has circumstantial suspicion, not evidence establishing why investigators selected or omitted witnesses. |
| [The group should have shared accountability](/questions/khawaja/shared-accountability/) | Broader cultural responsibility has substantial institutional and participant support. What justice required beyond it remains a reasoned moral judgment: acknowledgement, differentiated sanctions, institutional change and personal support need separate consideration. |
| [A shared response would have changed the bans](/questions/khawaja/alternative-sanctions/) | A plausible but untested counterfactual. The proportionality dispute has concrete comparative material; the prediction about a whole-team response is materially less established. |

## Related claims

These concern culture, continuing harm and experience of exclusion. They do not establish sandpaper knowledge and should not serve as character tests for witnesses.

| Specific claim | Where the captured record stands |
| --- | --- |
| [Ball manipulation and a win-at-all-costs culture preceded the scandal](/questions/khawaja/culture/) | There is substantial convergence on a broader cultural problem. The legality and causal role of each practice still require event-specific evidence and the applicable historical rule. Culture alone cannot decide who knew of sandpaper. |
| [The scandal still hangs over Bancroft](/questions/khawaja/bancroft-harm/) | Continuing stigma has firsthand support. A stronger claim that the scandal caused a particular non-selection remains decision-specific and weakly established by runs or suspicion alone. |
| [What evidence did Khawaja give that race cost him the Test captaincy?](/questions/khawaja/race-and-captaincy/) | Khawaja attributed the decision to race in his book. The chairman’s reported remark questioned his suitability for the job. Khawaja supplied the racial interpretation and later acknowledged he had no concrete evidence for it. |

## Missing evidence, in order of usefulness

- The complete relevant book chapter and, if made available, Khawaja’s reported 2018 notes and their provenance could sharpen several claims at once.
- Specific responses to named conversations or instructions could test individual knowledge more directly than general denials or cultural comments.
- The investigation’s remit, witness selection and records would matter to completeness and intent. Public availability remains unestablished.

Fuller historical source formats move up only when a particular omission could change an argument. Marsh’s published refusal is already captured; finding more of the same exchange is low priority. The [coverage page](/about/coverage/) is the continuing evidence to-do list.
