Cricket Australia investigated the Cape Town incident in 2018 and sanctioned Steve Smith, David Warner and Cameron Bancroft. In 2026, Usman Khawaja’s account raised questions about earlier use of sandpaper and wider responsibility. What would it take to settle those questions? What would it take for the players, the team and the public to move on?

## What does loyalty to the team require?

Did Khawaja break trust by making his allegations public? If responsibility extended beyond the three sanctioned players, what would loyalty have required of the rest of the team? What did Khawaja owe the teammates he was defending and those he implicated?

## What justifies looking again?

The 2018 investigation addressed the Cape Town incident. In 2026, Cricket Australia announced a confidential forum to hear further accounts, with no new sanctions. What in the later accounts is new to the public record? What might the original investigation never have examined? What evidence would justify correcting the record, acknowledging a wider failure, or imposing a new consequence?

## What does Cummins owe as captain today?

Cape Town was Pat Cummins’s 13th Test. He played as a bowler. What could he have known or done in that role? What does he owe the team and the public now as captain? When people call for him to stand down, is the case about what he is alleged to have done in 2018, what he has said since, or both? What evidence would make that case?

## When is an apology enough?

When a scandal returns on social media, does the renewed attention bring forward testimony that was missed, or ask people to answer the same allegation to a new audience? When have the standards or demands changed? Would an answer, an apology or a change in how cricket is run satisfy the demand this time? Who gets to say it has been satisfied?
