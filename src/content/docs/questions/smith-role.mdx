## The claim and its scope

Knowledge, failure to prevent, approval, concealment and misleading explanations are distinct forms of responsibility. A person can decline direct participation while still failing in a leadership duty.

This page presents archive analysis based on the cited sources. It distinguishes what a witness says, what an institution found and what can be inferred from either.

## Arguments for the claim

### The CA charges distinguish his role from Warner’s

Smith’s charges concern knowledge of a potential plan and failure to prevent it, whereas Warner’s include development, instruction and demonstration. This is evidence that CA did not publish identical allegations against them.

**Limit:** The same charge list also attributes concealment and misleading explanations to Smith. Different charges do not amount to exoneration or prove that he lacked authority to stop it.

**Read the passages:** [SP005 · Warner instructed Bancroft to use sandpaper on the ball](/documents/sp005-28-march-ca-public-findings-charges-and-sanctions/): [p-009](/documents/sp005-28-march-ca-public-findings-charges-and-sanctions/#p-009) · [p-011](/documents/sp005-28-march-ca-public-findings-charges-and-sanctions/#p-011)

### Bancroft recalls Smith explicitly distancing himself

Bancroft says Smith entered the Port Elizabeth discussion, wanted no part in it and distanced himself; he recalls a later similar response. This supports knowledge plus disengagement rather than direct physical implementation.

**Limit:** It is a later recollection and does not establish whether the disengagement was a sufficient intervention or what Smith knew at each later stage.

## Arguments against or limiting the claim

### Smith originally accepted a leadership-group decision

His initial account described the leadership group’s idea and accepted responsibility. The ICC records his admission that he was party to a decision to attempt an unfair alteration.

**Limit:** The initial public account was subsequently challenged as misleading. It is necessary to establish what he meant by leadership group rather than assume every senior player participated.

### Distancing oneself does not answer prevention and concealment

CA’s public charges include failure to prevent implementation, directing concealment and misleading officials and the public. Those allegations challenge a reading of disengagement as no meaningful involvement.

**Limit:** The complete underlying investigation records are missing; the source establishes the public charge, not independent observation of each alleged act.

**Read the passages:** [SP005 · Warner instructed Bancroft to use sandpaper on the ball](/documents/sp005-28-march-ca-public-findings-charges-and-sanctions/): [p-008](/documents/sp005-28-march-ca-public-findings-charges-and-sanctions/#p-008) · [p-009](/documents/sp005-28-march-ca-public-findings-charges-and-sanctions/#p-009)

### His full return-home statement accepts a failure of leadership

Smith describes allowing it to happen, calls it a failure of his own leadership and declines to blame Warner. This counters an interpretation that a limited role meant no responsibility.

**Limit:** Accepting leadership responsibility does not specify that he personally developed the sandpaper method or demonstrate every act alleged in the charges.

## What would help resolve it

- Compare the now-captured original questions and answers with underlying investigation records when available; different admissions have different scopes.
- What specific acts did Smith take to stop or facilitate the plan?
- Separate judgments of captaincy responsibility from claims about physical tampering or devising the method.
