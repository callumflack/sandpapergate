## What a source page means

One canonical page represents one underlying source. It records author or speaker, publication and event dates where known, original URL, the access URL actually consulted, collection status and limits. Publisher reporting is labelled as reporting; a complete article is not automatically a complete interview.

## Collection and extraction

Original HTML, PDFs and extracted text are retained privately. This edition provides original analysis, source metadata and citation locators. Source-specific extraction limits appear on every document page. Publisher framing and verbatim material are distinguished where the extraction supports it. Original recordings have not yet been watched or transcription-checked.

## Mirrors and access copies

Keep the original publisher URL and the consulted copy. Compare versions before claiming a mirror is complete or identical. Copies of one statement are one source, not independent corroboration. Research access is separate from permission to publish the full text.

## Dates

Preserve event dates, interview dates, publication dates and updates separately. Where publisher metadata conflicts with a URL or another page, mark the uncertainty. Do not silently turn a 2026 recollection into a contemporary 2018 record.

## Reading later recollections

In *History of the Peloponnesian War* 1.22, Thucydides describes testing his own impressions and the reports of others. He traces disagreements between eyewitnesses to “imperfect memory” and “undue partiality for one side or the other”. [Thucydides 1.22.2–3, translated by Richard Crawley](https://beta.perseus.tufts.edu/urn:cts:greekLit:tlg0003.tlg001.perseus-eng6/chapter:1.22/)

> Memory is especially unreliable when it comes to recalling past beliefs.

> all remembering of distant events involves reconstructive processes

[Leggatt J, *Gestmin SGPS SA v Credit Suisse (UK) Ltd*, paragraphs 18 and 21](https://www.financialinstitutionslegalsnapshot.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/23/2024/12/Gestmin-SGPS-SA-v-Credit-Suisse-UK-Ltd-Anor-2013-EWHC-3560-Comm-15-November-2013-1.pdf)

> emotions and objectivity are somewhat ill bedfellows.

Lord Saville said this while explaining how the Bloody Sunday Inquiry used contemporary witness statements, recordings and reporting to test later recollections. He noted that playing the recordings often confirmed what witnesses had just told the inquiry. [Lord Saville’s evidence to the Northern Ireland Affairs Committee, Q5–6](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm201012/cmselect/cmniaf/499/499i.pdf)

When reading a later account of Sandpapergate, ask what the person says they saw or heard at the time, what they believed then, and what they infer now. What contemporary record could test that account? Ask the same questions of an allegation and a denial. [Who knew, and when?](/questions/prior-knowledge/)

## Stable citations

Each source has an SP identifier. Captured paragraphs have links such as #p-001 on the source page. These numbers refer to the private captured edition, not the publisher’s numbering. The JSON manifest lists paragraph URLs and the capture-text hash. Cite the original publisher together with the relevant captured-edition locator. The locators do not mean the underlying source text is republished here.

## Interpretation

Separate source testimony, institutional findings, observed footage and our inference. A denial, lack of recollection, new detail and contradiction are different things. Question pages now set out source-linked arguments and their limits. They do not prescribe a verdict or promise equal evidence on each side. Passage-link integrity is checked automatically; the strength of an inference requires reading the source in context.
