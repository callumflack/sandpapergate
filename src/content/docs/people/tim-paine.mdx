## Accounts and responses

Bancroft’s later account names Paine as someone he spoke to about how the ball was moving. This is an allegation of conversations, not independent proof of Paine knowing sandpaper was being used. [Read Bancroft’s passage](/documents/sp031-bancroft-long-form-statement-original-publication/#p-079).

Paine’s 2022 memoir, as quoted in [‘My heart sank’: Paine reveals anguish of Sandpapergate scandal](/documents/sp082-my-heart-sank-paine-reveals-anguish-of-sandpapergate-scandal/), describes shock at seeing the sandpaper on the screen. Other published memoir passages reject the inference that everybody must know what happens in a dressing room, while asking whether owning the incident as a team would have helped the punished players. [Shared knowledge](/documents/sp083-tim-paine-comes-clean-over-infamous-cape-town-cricket-ball-tampering-saga-everyone-was-a-part-of-it/#p-009) and [collective responsibility](/documents/sp083-tim-paine-comes-clean-over-infamous-cape-town-cricket-ball-tampering-saga-everyone-was-a-part-of-it/#p-006) are different claims.

These are selected memoir passages and interview quotations in complete publisher articles, not the full memoir or a new response to Bancroft’s detailed 2026 allegations. Paine’s quoted denial of a planning meeting must not be treated as a categorical denial of a later post-exposure meeting.
